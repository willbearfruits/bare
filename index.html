A synthesizer that is the whole computer.

It starts from a USB stick straight into the instrument, with no operating system underneath, on PCs from the late 1990s to now.

Put it on a stick The manual PDF

One of the four splashes it plays as it starts: GENDY, after Xenakis's dynamic stochastic synthesis.

NO OPERATING SYSTEM

BARE! is the program the PC starts, and nothing else runs: the keys, the screen and the sound chip belong to the instrument.

OLD PCS AND NEW

Any PC since the Pentium Pro, from 32 MB of memory up, BIOS or UEFI. It sizes itself to what it finds.

YOUR WORK ON THE STICK

It runs from memory, so the stick can come out while you play. Projects, songs and your own instruments are saved on it.

An omnichord you play like the real one, a lineage of homages with their histories, and Doom's first level as breakcore. This is the first release, a beta: it works, and it is still being tried on more machines.

F1 PLAY

An OM-108 on the keyboard

PLAY is laid out after Suzuki's OM-108 and its owner's manual. The three letter rows are its MAJOR, MINOR and 7th chord buttons, pressed together for maj7, m7, dim, aug, sus4 and add9; the bottom row and the touchpad are its 13-string strumplate.

Ten voices, ten rhythms that change at the next bar, AUTO chords and bass, HOLD, SYNC START, a keyboard mode with drums, and MIDI out on its channels.

Try it: hold 6 and run a finger along the keys from Z to /.

F11 LINEAGE

Six homages, each with its history

Instruments after the people who made the music: Iannis Xenakis, with five views of his own; Murzin's ANS synthesizer, with a drone sketch after Coil; Steve Reich's phasing; Wendy Carlos's alpha, beta and gamma scales; Éliane Radigue's slow drones; Merzbow's noise from junk.

Each one plays, and each has a short history beside it: who, how, and what to listen to. TOUCH has one too: Michel Waisvisz and his Crackle Box.

F12 LINEAGE › XENAKIS

Metastaseis

In 1954 Iannis Xenakis drew 46 string glissandi as straight lines on graph paper, and together they made curved surfaces — the same surfaces became the Philips Pavilion.

Here you set two guide lines, the strings are strung between them, and the surface turns in 3D while it sounds. Enter writes it onto UPIC's page.

F2 SEQ · Shift+4

E1M1, as breakcore

The tracker reads Doom's first-level music from the WAD on your stick — yours, or Freedoom's — and lays it out on its grid: guitars and bass under chopped breakbeats at 172 BPM, snare rolls, stutters, a breakdown and a tape stop.

The notes come from your WAD; the arrangement is BARE!'s. The picture is Freedoom's E1M1.

  • Keys follow the chordShift+K: hold a chord on PLAY, and what you play on the other pages lands on it.

You need a USB stick of 256 MB or more — everything on it is replaced — and a PC that can start from USB.

  1. Download the image

    bare-1.0-beta.img.zip 6.8 MB bare-1.0-beta.img.xz 2.6 MB

    The same image twice: the .zip for Windows and macOS, the .xz for Linux. There is also an ISO for a CD or a virtual machine and a floppy (neither keeps projects), and SHA256SUMS.

  2. Write it to the stick

    WINDOWS

    1. Get balenaEtcher, Rufus or Raspberry Pi Imager.
    2. Choose the .zip you downloaded (in Raspberry Pi Imager: Choose OS, then Use custom), then the stick, and write.
    3. Windows may then offer to format the stick: say no. It can't read the part where BARE! keeps projects, and doesn't need to.

    MACOS

    1. Get balenaEtcher or Raspberry Pi Imager.
    2. In Etcher: Flash from file and the .zip you downloaded, Select target and the stick, Flash. (In Raspberry Pi Imager: Choose OS, then Use custom.)
    3. If macOS then says a disk isn't readable, choose Ignore: that is where BARE! keeps projects.

    LINUX

    One line downloads the image and writes it. First find the stick: lsblk lists the disks, and the one with your stick's size is it, sdb for example.

    curl -L https://github.com/willbearfruits/bare/releases/download/v1.0-beta/bare-1.0-beta.img.xz | xz -dc | sudo dd of=/dev/sdX bs=4M conv=fsync status=progress

    Put the stick's name where sdX is. Whatever disk you name is overwritten.

    Or balenaEtcher with the .zip, as on the other systems.

    Why not straight from this page? A web page can't write to a USB stick: browsers keep disks out of its reach, on purpose, and their USB access leaves storage devices out. So it takes one of these tools, once.

  3. Start the computer from it

    Turn Secure Boot off in the firmware settings. Plug the stick in and pick it from the boot menu: usually F12, F9, F8 or Esc at power-on.

  4. Play

    When the splash plays, press any key. Hold 6 and run a finger along the keys from Z to /: a C major chord, strummed. The F keys open the pages; Shift+? shows every key.

Already have a BARE! stick? Put BARE.UPD in its root and start from it: it updates itself and keeps your projects.

Got a floppy drive?

BARE! fits on one 3.5" 1.44 MB diskette. It starts a PC that has a BIOS and a floppy drive, an internal one or a USB drive the BIOS boots from, and plays like the stick, but can't save.

bare-1.0-beta-floppy.img 1.5 MB
  1. Linux: sudo dd if=bare-1.0-beta-floppy.img of=/dev/fd0 bs=18k. A USB floppy drive is a /dev/sdX: lsblk shows which.
  2. Windows: RawWrite for Windows: choose the image and drive A:, then Write.
  3. macOS, with a USB floppy drive: diskutil list finds it, diskutil unmountDisk /dev/diskN, then sudo dd if=bare-1.0-beta-floppy.img of=/dev/rdiskN bs=18k.
  4. Put the floppy first in the BIOS boot order and start the PC. The drive reads for about a minute, then the splash plays.

Booted in emulators so far. GRUB starts it: Limine, the stick's boot loader, can't read floppy drives.

PLAYED ONA ThinkPad X250 and an ASUS VivoBook (Intel 12th gen), BIOS and UEFI. It boots on 90 emulated PCs, from a Pentium II with 32 MB to 4K screens.
NOT TRIED YETOn real hardware: AC'97, the Sound Blaster 16, SATA and NVMe disks, the installer and Ethernet. Tried it? Say how it went.

A different splash each time it starts: a short piece, picture and sound.

An instrument is a text file on the stick: what it sounds like, and how it is played — the letter rows, the touchpad as a strip or a grid of pads, an arpeggio, a drone. Five come with it. SHRUTI is a shruti box: its keys open reeds tuned in just intonation, and a finger moving on the touchpad pumps the bellows.

# SHRUTI.TXT, less its comments
name: SHRUTI
[sound]
wave: pulse   width: 22   detune: 8
attack: 160   release: 500
[play]
keys: chromatic C3
tuning: just
hold: toggle
bellows: yes

The format: INSTRUMENTS.md.

Type iddqd on any page and it runs Doom, with the WAD on your stick: your own, or Freedoom's. The F keys go back to the synthesizer, and Doom waits. Its music is played by BARE!'s own voices.

38 A4 pages: every page of the instrument with its keys, the instrument files, which machines, and the stick. Made to be printed.

  • Hardware controllers made for BARE! Boxes of keys, pads, knobs and encoders laid out for its pages — an omnichord's chord buttons and strum plate, a tracker's step keys — built on RP2040 or ESP32-S3 boards, talking USB MIDI.
