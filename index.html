A synthesizer that is the whole computer.

It starts from a USB stick straight into the instrument, with no operating system underneath, on PCs from the late 1990s to now.

Download 2.5 beta The manual (PDF) Source

Recorded from the instrument itself: the GENDY splash, UPIC, ANS, SHRUTI, TOUCH, FX, and Doom.

Twelve pages, one for each F key. Everything plays at once: a song on the tracker, a chord held on PLAY, the tape rolling, the camera reading the ANS plate. It runs from memory, so once it has started the stick can come out.

F1PLAY

An omnichord: chord buttons on the number row, a strum plate on the letters and the touchpad, a rhythm section with a bass that follows the chord.
F2SEQ

A tracker: patterns of eight channels, an order list, effect columns, every note on the exact sample.
F3WAVE

A Fairlight-style page, green on black: waves drawn with the touchpad, harmonics, a 3D stack of them, and a sampler.
F4STRETCH

Freezes the phrase you just played and stretches it, up to a thousand times slower, while you play on.
F5OPERATOR

Four-operator FM: eight algorithms, envelopes, an LFO, four patches to play anywhere.
F6TAPE

An eight-track tape with reels, wow and hiss: record, overdub, loop, bounce, build whole songs.
F7FILE

Projects on the stick, songs out as WAV, MIDI and Ableton Link, the log, and an installer.
F8MIX

A strip for every source, sends to an echo and a reverb, and a filter, drive and crusher on the master.
F9TOUCH

A crackle box: eight bare pads of a small circuit. Two fingers close it, and pressing harder bends it.
F10FX

Effects to play live over a song: repeat, reverse, tape stop, gate, a dub throw, a reverb freeze.
F11ANS

Murzin's ANS: a plate of 360 tones read by a moving slit. Draw on it, or let the camera be the score.
F12XENAKIS

Four of Xenakis's ways of making music: UPIC's drawn arcs, GENDY, stochastic clouds and sieves.

An instrument is a text file on the stick: what it sounds like, and how it is played — the letter rows, the touchpad as a strip or a grid of pads, an arpeggio, a drone. Five come with it: STYLO, THEREMIN, GRIDPADS, SIEVHARP and SHRUTI.

SHRUTI is a shruti box, the drone box of Indian music. Its keys open reeds tuned in just intonation, and a finger moving to and fro on the touchpad pumps the bellows.

# the whole of SHRUTI.TXT, less its comments
name: SHRUTI
[sound]
wave: pulse   width: 22   detune: 8
attack: 160   release: 500
[play]
keys: chromatic C3
tuning: just
hold: toggle
bellows: yes

Type iddqd on any page and it runs Doom, with the WAD on your stick: your own, or Freedoom's. The F keys go back to the synthesizer, and Doom waits. Its music is played by BARE!'s own voices.

Six and a half minutes of all of it, recorded from the instrument itself, picture and sound together.

2.5 is a beta. It needs a PC with a Pentium Pro or later.

  1. Download bare-2.5.img.xz from the 2.5 release.
  2. Write it to a USB stick of 256 MB or more with balenaEtcher or Raspberry Pi Imager, or on Linux with xz -dc bare-2.5.img.xz | sudo dd of=/dev/sdX bs=4M conv=fsync. Everything on the stick is replaced.
  3. Turn Secure Boot off, plug the stick in and start the computer from it: the boot menu is usually F12, F9, F8 or Esc at power-on.
  4. When the splash plays, press any key. Hold 4 and run a finger along the keys from A to ': a C major chord, strummed.
PLAYED ONA ThinkPad X250 and an ASUS VivoBook (Intel 12th gen), BIOS and UEFI. It boots on 89 emulated PCs, from a Pentium II with 32 MB to 4K screens.

  • Hardware controllers made for BARE! Boxes of keys, pads, knobs and encoders laid out for its pages — an omnichord's chord buttons and strum plate, a tracker's step keys — built on RP2040 or ESP32-S3 boards, talking USB MIDI.
